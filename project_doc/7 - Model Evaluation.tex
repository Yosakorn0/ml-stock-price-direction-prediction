\chapter{MODEL EVALUATION}

\section{Evaluation Strategy: Multi-Asset Performance}
Model performance is assessed across MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, Gold, and Bitcoin datasets using time-series walk-forward validation. We compare two distinct configurations to scientifically measure the contribution of sentiment polarity:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Technical Baseline}: Using only OHLCV and technical indicators (RSI, MACD).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Source Hybrid}: Integrating Technical, Macro (FRED), and FinBERT Sentiment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scientific Performance Metrics}
The comparative analysis of metrics is presented in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:model_comparison}. Unlike traditional accuracy, we prioritize the F1-score and **Out-of-Sample (OOS) Sharpe Ratio** to measure generalizability. While behavioral sentiment provides a 1--3\% accuracy lift, its primary value is in reducing the "entropy" of the predictive signals during news-heavy market regimes.

\section{Trust and Risk Analysis: OOS Sharpe Ratios}
To establish "Scientific Trust" and avoid the pitfalls of evaluation bias, we strictly isolate the final performance metrics to the **Out-of-Sample (Test) set**, which comprises the last 20\% of the data chronological. Furthermore, the annualized Sharpe ratio calculation includes a 0.1\% transaction cost per trade. This rigorous "Reality Check" prevents the reporting of inflated metrics caused by train-set leakage or unrealistic execution assumptions (see Appendix Table~\ref{tab:sharpe_ratios}).
